See, edit and measure your Claude Code agents

ai-office turns your agents, skills and slash commands into a pixel-art office and a live graph. See how your team is organised, rewire it without fear, and measure which agents and skills actually do the work.

Walk through the demo office Install in one command

Free and open source (MIT). Runs on your machine from ~/.claude; nothing leaves it.

What ai-office does

See

Every agent, skill and command you have written, laid out as rooms of people who work together and as a graph of who calls whom. Each link quotes the file and line it comes from.

Edit

Wire an agent to a skill by dragging, change its model or colour, retire skills, archive agents and compose slash commands on a canvas. Every write shows the diff first.

Measure

Cross what your prompts promise with what your transcripts prove: who gets delegated, which skills get invoked, what the idle ones cost in context, and live activity as it happens.

See your setup as it really is

Two views of the same files, switchable without losing your selection. Nothing is a hand-drawn diagram: every room, shelf and line comes from something written in ~/.claude.

The Office

A floor plan in isometric pixel art. Rooms are not invented themes: they come from which skills each agent cites and which commands call it, so every door carries the name of a real skill or command and tells you why those people share it.

Position is data. A grey figure has never been delegated. The height of a book spine is how established a skill is; a blank spine is a skill nobody has opened. The archive of skills that half the floor cites faces the storeroom of skills nobody claims.

The Blueprint

The graph with every layer at once. Dotted is what your prompts declare; solid is what your transcripts prove. Select anything and the side panel quotes the file and line behind each claim.

Press / to find any agent, skill or command, and ? for the legend and shortcuts.

Edit it without fear

Everything you edit goes through the same door: the diff is shown before the disk is touched, a backup is kept, and a line lands in an audit log. Nothing is ever deleted.

  • Draw a relation. Drag from an agent to a skill in the Blueprint and the citation is written into its prompt.
  • Retire a skill. One click sets disable-model-invocation. Reversible, and the fastest way to raise your active ratio.
  • Compose a slash command. Pick who takes part, in what order, consulting what. Steps in the same phase run in parallel, and the preview is the exact Markdown that will be written.
  • Archive an agent. It moves aside, not to the bin, and comes back with one click.

Measure what actually works

Your prompts say who should do what. Your transcripts say who did. The census crosses both in a few seconds, even over gigabytes of sessions, and the same numbers live in the interface, each one citing its source. 

$ npx ai-office census

  agents               14   6 have not worked in the last 90 days
  skills               28   14 unused in the last 90 days
  custom commands       8   4 run at least once

  active ratio    32%
  Goes up when you use what you have or retire what you won't.

  the graph you wrote, and the one that exists
  agent→skill cited in their prompt   41  declared
  agent→skill proven                  13  in the history
  1. 6 of 14 agents did nothing in 90 days. You wrote their prompts. No session in the last 90 days delegated anything to them.
  2. Half the skills, idle. Their descriptions still load into every session and cost tokens.
  3. Active ratio: 32%. It goes up when you use what you have, or retire what you won't. Never by spending more.
  4. 41 promises, 13 proofs. Agents cite skills in their prompts. Transcripts show which citations turned into calls. That gap is the product.

Numbers from the demo installation that ships with the project. Run npx ai-office demo to explore it yourself.

The graph is already written, in prose

Agents tell themselves to “read the style-guide skill first”. Commands name the agents they call. None of that is a structured field, so no tool sees it. ai-office extracts it, with the file and line that justify each link, and lays it over what your transcripts prove.

Available
installed skills Anyone can invoke it, if the model decides to.
Assigned
prose in the agent's prompt Its prompt says so. Nobody guarantees it.
Preloaded
skills: in the frontmatter Always loaded, whatever it costs.
Proven
your transcripts It did it, N times, the last one on a given day. This layer wins: it is the only one backed by facts.

Watch your agents clock in

With the hooks installed, the office comes alive while you work in another window: agents materialise when they are delegated and a skill's spine lights up when it is invoked. Unlike a session viewer, each of those events leaves a permanent mark on the records, so watching the work changes what things are.

Principles

Nothing leaves your machine

No server, no telemetry, no account, no network. It reads ~/.claude and a local daemon listens on 127.0.0.1 only, behind a token that changes on every start.

Every number cites its source

If a figure cannot say which file and field it comes from, it is not shown. We even document the metric we tried and threw away because the data did not back it.

No number improves by spending more

A skill's grade saturates: the 50th use is worth no more than the 3rd, and it expires after 90 days without use.

Install

Requires Node 22.18 or newer. Nothing is written to your configuration without showing you the diff first.

In one command

Nothing to install globally. Opens the office in your browser.

npx ai-office          # the office
npx ai-office census   # the numbers
npx ai-office demo     # a fictional office, safe to edit

As a Claude Code plugin

For live activity. The hooks are declared by the plugin, so your settings.json is never touched. Adds the /census command.

claude plugin marketplace add alex-roc/ai-office
claude plugin install ai-office@ai-office

Interface and CLI in English and Spanish: switch with the EN/ES button or --lang es.
